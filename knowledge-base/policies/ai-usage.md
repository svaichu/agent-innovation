# Use of AI tools

## Allowed
- The company AI assistant (business plan, no training on our data) for drafting, summarising, translation and code help.
- Public and internal information in that assistant.

## Not allowed
- Personal data or customer contracts in any AI tool without approval from E113 (Data Protection Officer).
- Private AI accounts for company work.
- **Decisions about people (hiring, performance, promotion, dismissal) made or ranked by AI.** A tool may organise information; a person decides.
- Sending AI-generated text to customers without a human reading it.
- Inferring emotion or stress of employees from camera, microphone or keyboard data.

## Proposing a new AI use case
1. Describe the problem, intended purpose, users and affected people, deployment context, data, expected benefit, effort and human oversight.
2. The idea is checked for overlap with existing ideas and projects.
3. It is screened for AI suitability: would conventional automation be enough?
4. It is screened against the EU AI Act (see below) and for data protection.
5. The leadership sync decides on the ranked portfolio. **Tools may recommend; people decide.**

Ottilie Janssens (E26) keeps the register of proposed and live AI use cases.

## EU AI Act: our screening obligations
Every proposed AI use case gets a preliminary screening before it is funded. The screening is advisory and is confirmed by Jolanda Esposito (E113) and Tobias Haas (E110) before anything goes live.

| What we check | Reference | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Prohibited practices | Article 5 | The idea is not pursued, whatever its business value |
| High-risk classification | Article 6 and Annexes I and III | Qualified legal review and a conformity plan before any build |
| Transparency obligations | Article 50 | Disclosure to the person interacting with the system; can also apply on top of high-risk |
| Minimal or no risk | - | Recorded only when the available information supports it |

Known high-risk areas for us: anything touching **employment and worker management** (Annex III, point 4), including CV screening, task allocation and performance monitoring. Our own policy forbids these regardless.

Customer-facing assistants and chatbots fall under the Article 50 transparency obligation: the customer must be told they are dealing with an AI system.
